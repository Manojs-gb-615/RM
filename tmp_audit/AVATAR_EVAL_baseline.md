## Method

- Runs the same TypeScript retargeter the browser uses (`src/avatar/retarget.ts`) in Node.
- Reprojection compares forward-kinematics joints to the input canonical keypoints in 2D. Positions are already in canonical units (neck origin, per-clip median shoulder width = 1), so distances are in shoulder-width (SW) units. `rest` columns are the identity/rest-pose baseline, included to show the solver is doing something.
- Jitter is mean absolute second difference of selected body and hand keypoints, measured before and after One Euro filtering.
- Depth flips are counted by the solver (sign of inferred z changes between frames). Large rotation jumps count frames where any local bone rotates more than 60Â° from the previous frame.
- Proximity counts frames where fingertips of opposite hands, hand-to-head, or hand-behind-torso events occur. ASL uses contact, so these are diagnostics, not failures.
- Bone-length consistency is FK length versus rest length for every bone.
- Golden hashes are FNV-1a over local quaternions quantized to 1e-4, sampled every 4 frames.

## What these numbers do and do not show

- They show that 2D reprojection stays bounded, filtering reduces jitter, bone lengths stay constant, and the solver is deterministic (golden).
- They do **not** show ASL accuracy, naturalness, or correctness of inferred depth. 2Dâ†’3D depth is under-determined; the sign is chosen by a forward prior and hysteresis.
- They are measured on synthetic motions, not human signers. Real How2Sign clips and a real VRM must be checked manually before making any claim about signing quality.
- The mannequin/rest skeleton proportions differ from a real VRM; per-avatar reprojection should be re-run with an avatar-specific skeleton later.
